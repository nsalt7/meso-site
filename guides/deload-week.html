Deload weeks: when and how

A deload is a planned easy week. You keep training, on the same days and the same lifts, but with fewer sets, a little less weight, and plenty left in the tank. The aim is to let fatigue clear so the fitness you built over the mesocycle can show.

Cut the effort, keep the pattern

A Delphi panel of strength and physique coaches and sport scientists agreed that a deload means reducing training stress, not stopping training (Bell 2023). That matters: in a supervised trial, a full week off part way through a program cost some strength while leaving muscle growth unaffected (Coleman 2024). A deload that reduced volume, rather than stopping, did not blunt growth (Pancar 2026, in untrained men).

A practical deload week:

  • Half the sets. If you did 4 sets of a lift, do 2.
  • About 10% less load. Enough to make it easy, not so much the movement feels foreign.
  • Lots in reserve. Stop every set around 4 reps short of failure. See reps in reserve.
  • Same days, same exercises. Keep the rhythm and the skill. A deload is not the week to try a new variation.

The exact cuts (half, 10%, 4 in reserve) are convention, consistent with the consensus rather than tested head to head. The principle, reduce the stress and keep the pattern, is the evidence.

When to deload: on schedule

Most lifters deload at the end of each block, every four to six weeks of hard training. That interval is convention, chosen so fatigue never gets far ahead of you. It also gives you a natural line between blocks: a fresh start where you set the next block a little below the volume you peaked at and earn the climb again.

When to deload: early, when the logbook says so

There is no reliable blood test for overreaching. The recognised sign is a sustained drop in performance (Meeusen 2013; Bell 2020). One bad day is noise: poor sleep, a busy week, a cold. Two weeks running of falling numbers across several muscles is a pattern, and that is the moment to pull the deload forward rather than grind on.

Signals that should not trigger a deload on their own: missing a pump, or feeling a bit flat once. Soreness tracks damage and novelty more than growth (Damas 2016; Damas 2018), so it is a reason to hold volume, not a scoreboard.

Deload or rest?

If you are sick or injured, rest and recover; that is a different decision. If you are simply tired from a hard block, the deload is the better tool: you keep the habit and the movement patterns, and you come back with less strength to rebuild.

How Meso handles it

The final week of every Meso block is a deload: half the sets, about 10% off the load you actually used, 4 reps in reserve, same days and exercises. If your best sets regress two weeks running on two or more muscles, Meso brings the deload forward and ends the block a week early. Meso's two-week, two-muscle threshold is its own convention, built on the evidence that a sustained drop is the signal.

Let the logbook decideMeso runs these rules for you: log your sets and it writes next week from what you actually lifted. Works offline, no account.
