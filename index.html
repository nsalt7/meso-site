Volume you have to earn.

Meso reads what you actually lifted and writes next week from it. Beat last week and it adds a set. Stall and it holds. Every rule comes from published research, and you can read the papers in the app.

What it does

Tells you the weight

Open a session and the numbers are already there: the load, the rep window, and how many reps to leave in reserve. Lift, log what happened, and the next week is written from it.

Adds volume only when you earn it

Extra sets arrive when your logged performance improves. Soreness and joint pain can hold volume back or cut it, but they never add it, because feeling wrecked is not evidence of growth.

Shows its working

Every rule in the engine is cited to the literature, in plain language, inside the app. If you want to check whether the app is guessing, you can.

A look at it

The rules it runs on

  • Performance is the accelerator. Beat last week's best sets on a muscle and it earns one more weekly set; stall and it holds; regress and it drops (Mann 2010; Helms 2018; Enes 2024).
  • Soreness is only a brake. Soreness tracks damage and novelty, not growth, so it can hold volume back but never add it (Damas 2016; Damas 2018).
  • Reps first, then load. Climb the rep window, then add roughly 2.5 to 5 percent. Go heavier than asked and hold it, and that becomes your working weight (Plotkin 2022).
  • Failure is rationed. Sets ramp from 3 reps in reserve down to 1. Only isolation work touches failure, and only in the last hard week (Robinson 2024; Refalo 2023).
  • Deloads are scheduled, and early when the data says so. Two weeks of regression across several muscles pulls the deload forward, because a sustained performance drop is the validated overreaching signal (Bell 2023; Meeusen 2013).
